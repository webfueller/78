Antechamber

Rehearse the week before you live it.

Five minutes to set up. No account, no password handed over, and nothing leaves your computer — because there is nowhere for it to go.

Setting it up

  1. Install it

    You need Python 3.11 or newer. Nothing else — no accounts, no API keys, no services to sign up for.

    git clone https://github.com/webfueller/78 antechamber
cd antechamber
pip install -e .

  2. See what it does, with made-up data

    antechamber demo

    Open http://127.0.0.1:8787. You get a fictional person's inbox and calendar, already full. Press Rehearse the week.

    Five plans appear side by side, with a map of how each one probably goes and a recommendation. Click any ending on the map to read that week hour by hour. Commit it and you get a receipt; undo puts everything back.

    Nothing here is yours and nothing is kept. Delete the file it made when you are bored of it.

  3. Try it on one real conversation

    Go to http://127.0.0.1:8787/paste. Paste an email thread you are actually dreading — the kind with several "On Monday, Ana wrote:" lines still in it — put your own address in the box, and press Rehearse it.

    No account. Nothing saved: the copy it builds lives in memory for one second and is gone before the page finishes loading.

    With a single thread it does not know much about the other person yet, and it tells you that plainly instead of pretending.

  4. Point it at your own mail

    Two exports, both things you already have a right to.

    Your mail. Google Takeout → select Mail only → download. You get a file ending .mbox. Other providers have the same thing under "export".

    Your calendar. Google Calendar → Settings → Import & export → Export. You get a .ics file.

    antechamber --db mine.db import \
    --mbox ~/Downloads/All\ mail.mbox \
    --ics  ~/Downloads/calendar.ics \
    --me   you@example.com

antechamber --db mine.db serve

    Export your calendar again each week and keep the old files. One export shows where your meetings are; two show that one moved, and that is the only way it can learn who reschedules on you. Pass them all: --ics week1.ics --ics week2.ics

  5. The weekly habit

    Start it, choose what it should think about — chase what has gone quiet, cut what you do not use, defend the calendar — and press rehearse. Read the plans, take one or take none. Hold is a real answer and it is often the right one. Then commit, read the receipt, and keep your 24 hours to change your mind.

    Everything lives in the one .db file you named. Delete it and the product knows nothing about you. There is no cloud copy, because there is no cloud.

What makes it different

Every claim below is one that something in the repository checks, and each is followed by what checks it.

Nothing happens until you say so

You see the week play out on a copy first. Only the version you pick becomes real, it comes with a receipt, and you have a day to take it back. Most tools ask you to trust them; this one asks you to look.

The receipt proves the committed week is the rehearsed one, hash for hash

It never puts words in anyone's mouth

When it shows you that Ana probably replies, it is showing a probability and a likely time — never invented sentences with her name on them. Simulated people cannot escape the copy they live in.

Enforced by an allowlist in the store, not by a convention

It keeps score of its own guesses

Every rehearsal writes down what it expects, and later reality settles it. You can ask how often it has been right. Almost nothing else that predicts anything will show you that.

Beats its own baseline by 24–42% across five test worlds

Your mail stays on your machine

No OAuth, no password, no server. It reads files you exported yourself, and it listens only to your own computer. There is no account to delete because there was never one to make.

Refuses any request that did not come from your own browser

It tells you when it does not know

Thin evidence is labelled as thin. A guessed number says it is a guess. When it cannot see enough of your calendar to judge, it says so rather than printing a confident zero.

The scoring weights and their origin ship in every result

The picture is safe to post

The map you can save and share carries plan names, counts and probabilities — no contact, no subject line, no sentence anyone wrote. You can show someone your week without showing them your inbox.

A test reads the drawing code and fails if it ever touches a name

Two things you should know before you start

It does not send email yet

Committing writes down what you decided, and that is what makes the receipt and the undo real. The mail connection itself is not built. When it is, the undo has to change: you can take a row out of a file, and you cannot take back a message somebody has already read.

Most of the value arrives in the first fortnight

It finds the subscriptions you forgot and clears the conversations that went quiet — and that is a backlog, not a tap. After that it is worth roughly one extra answered message a week. That is measured rather than assumed, and you should know it before you build a habit around it.
